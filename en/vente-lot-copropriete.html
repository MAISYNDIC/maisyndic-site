Selling your flat in a co-ownership in Luxembourg

Before the deed is signed, the notary asks the syndic about the state of the accounts for the unit being sold. Here are the documents, the deadlines and who pays what, seller and buyer.

The syndic's certificate

When a unit is sold, the seller gives the notary a certificate from the syndic, less than one month old, confirming that they have no outstanding obligations towards the owners' association (article 25 of the law of 16 May 1975). If sums are still owed, the syndic instead provides a statement of these sums, which the notary can pay out of the sale price.

Without a certificate: notice of transfer and objection

If the certificate is not presented, the buyer must send the syndic a notice of transfer by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt. The syndic then has 8 days from receiving it to object to the payment of the price, by bailiff's writ, in order to obtain the sums the seller still owes. The objection states the amount of the claim and its grounds. In that case, no payment of the price can be relied on against the syndic. The same applies if the notice of transfer was never sent.

What the buyer may owe

The law makes the buyer jointly and severally liable with the seller for charges still owed for the closed financial year and for the months of the current financial year (article 25). This is why the buyer and their notary insist on the syndic's certificate: it saves the buyer from paying the seller's debts.

Splitting charges between seller and buyer

No law or regulation requires a pro rata calculation. Calls for funds are sent to the owner known to the syndic when they are issued; the deed of sale may provide for a different split, which seller and buyer settle between themselves, often at the notary's. The balance of the annual statement is in principle allocated to whoever owns the unit on the day the general meeting approves the accounts.

The works fund stays with the building

Contributions to the works fund are attached to the unit and belong permanently to the owners' association: they are not refunded to the seller (article 11bis). An interested buyer can check the amount held for the unit, which is often taken into account when negotiating the price. Our guide to the works fund.

Informing the syndic of the change of owner

Every sale is notified to the syndic without delay, by the parties or by the notary (article 31 of the Grand-Ducal regulation of 13 June 1975). From that notification onwards, the new owner receives the calls for funds and the general meeting notices.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the syndic's certificate valid?

It must be less than one month old when it is presented to the notary (article 25 of the law of 16 May 1975).

Does the seller get back what they paid into the works fund?

No. Contributions are attached to the unit and belong to the owners' association; they are not refunded when a unit is sold (article 11bis). Seller and buyer can take them into account in the price.

Who pays for works voted before the sale?

Calls for funds are sent to the owner known to the syndic at the time they are issued. The deed of sale may provide for a different split, settled directly between seller and buyer.
