New building: getting the first general meeting right

In a new building, everything has to be set up: syndic, budget, contracts, works fund. The first general meeting lays the foundations for years to come.

The syndic chosen by the developer must be ratified

The co-ownership rules drawn up by the developer often appoint a first syndic. The law requires this appointment to be submitted for ratification by the first general meeting (article 20 of the law of 16 May 1975). The co-owners can therefore confirm that syndic or choose another, by a majority of the votes of all co-owners (article 16). The syndic is appointed for three years at most (article 14). If there is no syndic, a co-owner can ask the president of the District Court to appoint one.

The meeting notice

The syndic calls the meeting at least 15 days in advance, with the agenda and the relevant documents (Grand-Ducal regulation of 13 June 1975, articles 3 and 5). Co-owners holding at least a quarter of the votes can ask the syndic to call it (article 2). Our guide to the general meeting.

What the first meeting decides

  • The syndic: ratification or appointment, length of the mandate, contract and fees.
  • The budget for the first year and the schedule of calls for funds.
  • The works fund: compulsory from the start, with a minimum of €3 per square metre per year for energy classes A+ to C, the most common case in new buildings. Our guide to the works fund.
  • Insurance for the building.
  • Maintenance contracts: lift, heating, cleaning, green spaces.
  • The owners' council, to assist the syndic and oversee its management (article 23).

Defects in the common parts and the guarantees

Cracks, leaks, a faulty garage door or lift: defects in the common parts should be noted early and reported to the developer in writing. The owners' association can take legal action to defend the building's rights (article 12). The developer and the builders are liable for defects for ten years for major structural work and two years for minor work (articles 1646-1, 1792 and 2270 of the Civil Code): hence the importance of acting before these periods expire.

The documents to collect

The syndic gathers from the developer: the co-ownership rules, the plans, the energy performance certificate, the instructions and guarantees for the equipment, the current contracts and the keys or badges for the common parts. These documents are needed throughout the life of the building.

Frequently asked questions

Do we have to keep the syndic chosen by the developer?

No. The first general meeting must ratify its appointment; it can also appoint a different syndic, by a majority of the votes of all co-owners (articles 16 and 20 of the law of 16 May 1975).

Is the works fund compulsory in a new building?

Yes. Every co-ownership pays an annual contribution to the works fund. For a new building in energy class A+ to C, the minimum is €3 per square metre of energy reference area per year.

How long do we have to report defects?

The statutory guarantees last ten years for major structural work and two years for minor work. It is best to report defects in writing as soon as they appear, without waiting for these periods to end.
