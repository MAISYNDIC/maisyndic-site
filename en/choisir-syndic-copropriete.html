How to choose a syndic in Luxembourg

A good syndic is judged on facts you can check, not on a brochure. Here are the criteria to compare, the questions to ask and how the syndic is appointed at the general meeting.

Key points

  • A professional syndic must hold a business permit as property manager – co-ownership syndic.
  • Compare written prices: flat fee, extras and minimum per building, worked out over a year.
  • Ask for the full contract before the meeting, not just a sales offer.
  • Check how owners will access the accounts, invoices and documents.
  • The syndic is appointed by the general meeting, by a majority of the votes of all owners.

An authorised syndic

In Luxembourg, managing co-ownerships on a professional basis requires a business permit as property manager – co-ownership syndic (administrateur de biens – syndic de copropriété). Ask the candidate for the permit number: it usually appears in their legal notice and in their contract. Also ask whether they hold professional liability insurance.

The only exception: an owner may act as a volunteer syndic, without a permit, if the building has no more than 9 residential units and they own one of them. The volunteer syndic and our Volunteer+ plan.

Written prices, compared over a year

Syndics usually charge a flat fee per unit or per home, sometimes a single amount for the whole building, with a minimum. Services outside the flat fee (extraordinary meeting, insurance claim, sale file, works supervision, taking over the records) can change the final bill a great deal.

To compare two offers, work everything out as an annual cost for your building: the flat fee, plus the extras you are likely to need during the year.

Example: 12 homes at €22 excl. VAT per home per month, i.e. 22 × 12 × 12 = €3,168 a year. With one unit sold during the year (file for the notary at €200) and one extraordinary meeting (€250), the real cost reaches €3,618 excl. VAT. An offer where these lines say "on request" cannot be compared.

What the flat fee really covers

Two flat fees at the same price can cover very different things. Get it in writing:

  • how many general meetings are included, how long they last and at what times;
  • how many visits to the building per year;
  • what contact is planned with the owners' council;
  • management of the works fund and allocation of water or heating costs;
  • reminders for unpaid charges;
  • online access to the accounts and documents.

Availability and languages

Ask who your contact person will be, how quickly they normally reply and how to reach the syndic in an emergency: a leak, a broken lift, an insurance claim. In Luxembourg buildings, owners often speak different languages: check in which languages meeting notices, statements and day-to-day exchanges are possible.

Transparent accounts

The co-ownership's money is not mixed with the syndic's: all sums received must be paid without delay into a bank account opened in the name of the owners' association (syndicat des copropriétaires). Also ask:

  • whether the owners' council will be able to view this account;
  • whether every invoice paid will be available with its supporting document;
  • whether the annual statement will be broken down unit by unit;
  • whether the minutes, contracts and co-ownership rules can be viewed at any time.

The contract: term, end of mandate, takeover

Ask for the full contract before the general meeting and read it before voting: term of the mandate, conditions for ending it, list of services and their prices. Also check what taking over your records will cost: it may be free, or charged if the previous syndic's bookkeeping is behind.

If you are leaving a syndic, the handover of records, accounts and funds needs preparing. Switching syndic, step by step.

Questions to ask before the vote

  • What is your business permit number?
  • What is the total annual cost for our building, including likely extras?
  • Which services are charged on top, and at what price?
  • How many general meetings and visits are included?
  • Who will be our contact, and how quickly do they reply?
  • How will we access the accounts, invoices and documents?
  • In which languages do you work?
  • What does taking over our records cost, and how does it work?

How the syndic is appointed

The syndic is appointed by the general meeting by a majority of the votes of all owners, whether present or not (article 16 of the law). If this majority is not reached, a new meeting may decide by a majority of the votes of the owners present or represented (article 15).

The item must be on the agenda, and the essential terms of the proposed contracts must be sent no later than with the meeting notice, which is served at least 15 days before the meeting. So ask for quotes early enough to enclose them.

In a new building, a syndic named in the co-ownership rules before the first meeting must be confirmed by that first general meeting. Our guide to the general meeting.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a syndic cost in Luxembourg?

Usually a monthly or annual flat fee per home, with a minimum per building, plus services charged separately. The price depends on the size of the building, its equipment and the state of its bookkeeping. At mäisyndic, prices are published: €10.90 excl. VAT per home per month for Volunteer+ (up to 9 homes), €24.90 for Essential and €29.90 for Complete, with lower rates for these two plans above 10 homes.

Can an owner be the syndic?

Yes, on a voluntary basis and without a business permit, if the building has no more than 9 residential units and they own one of them. Beyond that, or if they do not wish to take on the role, the co-ownership appoints a professional syndic.

Can we change syndic during the mandate?

The general meeting can dismiss the syndic and appoint a new one, by the article 16 majority. Check what the current contract provides for early termination.

Should we ask for several quotes?

Comparing two or three offers against the same criteria is the best way to decide. The owners' council can collect them and present them to the general meeting.
