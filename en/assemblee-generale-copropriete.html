The co-ownership general meeting

The general meeting is where co-owners decide together: budget, accounts, works, choice of syndic. Here is how it is called, how voting works and what happens next, under Luxembourg law.

Key points

  • At least one meeting a year, called by the syndic.
  • A notice sent at least 15 days in advance, with the agenda and the documents.
  • Votes are only taken on items on the agenda.
  • Three main majorities, depending on how important the decision is.
  • Two months to challenge a decision, from the date it is notified.

Who calls the meeting?

Every co-ownership holds at least one general meeting a year, called by the syndic. The owners' council, or one or more co-owners holding at least a quarter of the votes, can also require a meeting. They send their request to the syndic, stating the items to be put on the agenda. The co-ownership rules may set a lower threshold.

If the syndic has still not acted more than eight days after a formal notice, the chair of the owners' council may call the meeting themselves. If there is no owners' council, any co-owner may ask the president of the District Court to appoint a person to call it.

The notice: at least 15 days in advance

The notice states the place, date and time of the meeting, and the agenda, which sets out each item put to the vote. Except in urgent cases, it is notified at least 15 days before the meeting; the co-ownership rules may set a longer period. Unless those rules say otherwise, the meeting is held in the municipality where the building is located.

Depending on the items on the agenda, the following documents are sent at the latest with the agenda:

  • the accounts for the past financial year, the statement of debts and receivables and the cash position, for approval of the accounts;
  • the forecast budget, for the vote on the budget for the following financial year;
  • the essential terms of the proposed quotes and contracts, for works or a contract to be approved;
  • the draft text, to draw up or amend the co-ownership rules or the allocation of charges;
  • the draft resolution, in particular for a delegation of powers, alteration works or legal action.

Adding an item to the agenda

Within six days of the notice, any co-owner, or the owners' council, may ask the person who called the meeting to add new items. That person sends the list of these items to everyone at least five days before the meeting.

The meeting can only vote validly on items on the agenda, and only if the notice and documents were sent in accordance with the rules. The simplest approach: send your proposals to the syndic before the notice is prepared.

On the day of the meeting

An attendance sheet shows the name and address of each co-owner present or represented, and their number of votes. Each of them signs it, then the chair of the meeting certifies it. The meeting appoints this chair at the start and, if it wishes, a meeting committee. The syndic acts as secretary, unless the meeting decides otherwise.

Each co-owner has a number of votes equal to their share of the common parts, usually expressed in thousandths. If a co-owner holds more than half of the common parts, their votes are reduced to the total votes of the other co-owners: they cannot decide alone.

Can't attend? Any co-owner may be represented by a person of their choice. Give them a written proxy, dated and signed, that names the meeting concerned.

Where the co-ownership rules make only some co-owners pay certain expenses, for example the lift serving one entrance, only those co-owners vote on these expenses, with votes in proportion to their contribution.

Majorities: who decides what?

The majority required depends on how important the decision is. The law sets three main levels, and unanimity in a few cases.

Voting majorities at a co-ownership general meeting in Luxembourg
MajorityExamples of decisions
Majority of the votes of co-owners present or representedarticle 15 of the lawApproval of the accounts, vote on the budget, amount of the works fund contribution, the syndic's fees, and any decision for which the law sets no other majority.
Majority of the votes of all co-owners, present or notarticle 16Appointment or dismissal of the syndic and of the members of the owners' council; delegation of powers; authorising a co-owner to carry out, at their own expense, works that affect the common parts or the building's external appearance; improvement or alteration of existing equipment; energy renovation; service ducts; generation and storage of renewable energy in the common parts.
Majority of co-owners holding at least three quarters of the votesarticle 17Property acquisitions and acts of disposal, such as selling a common part; amendment of the co-ownership rules on the use, enjoyment and administration of the common parts; alteration, addition or improvement works other than those under article 16; setting up an owners' council not provided for in the rules.
Unanimityarticles 8, 18 and 30Change to the allocation of charges, except where the law provides otherwise; sale of common parts needed for the building's intended use; adding storeys or other construction by the co-ownership to create new units.

For article 16, the votes of all co-owners are counted, including those who are absent. If this majority is not reached, a new meeting may decide by the article 15 majority. If its agenda only includes items already on the agenda of the first meeting, it can be called eight days in advance, without sending the documents again.

Example: a building with 12 co-owners and 1,000 thousandths. To change syndic (article 16), more than 500 thousandths must vote in favour, whether or not the other co-owners are present. For alteration works (article 17), at least 7 co-owners must vote in favour, holding at least 750 thousandths between them.

If the votes are tied, the decision adopted is the one supported by the larger number of co-owners. If the number of co-owners is also tied, a new vote is held immediately; if there is another tie, any co-owner may refer the matter to the District Court.

Finally, no majority can force a co-owner to change the intended use of their unit or the conditions of enjoyment set for it in the co-ownership rules.

Deciding without a meeting: written consultation

For the maintenance and repair of the common parts, including replacing equipment that no longer works with a more efficient installation, the law allows decisions to be taken outside a meeting, by written consultation. The syndic must then give everyone all the relevant information, in particular the cost of the works. The owners' council, if there is one, must have authorised this procedure beforehand.

This is useful for a repair that does not justify bringing everyone together.

After the meeting: minutes and challenges

The minutes record the text of each decision and the result of each vote. They give the names of the co-owners who voted against, those who abstained and those who did not take part in the vote. A co-owner who voted against may ask for their reservations about the regularity of the proceedings to be recorded. The minutes are signed by the chair, the secretary and, where applicable, the members of the meeting committee, then kept in a register held by the syndic.

The syndic gives notice of the decisions to the co-owners who opposed them or who were neither present nor represented. They have two months from this notification to challenge a decision in court; after that, they can no longer do so. The law reserves this remedy for those who opposed the decision and those who were absent: if you disagree, vote against. For its part, the syndic carries out the decisions adopted.
