Heating and hot water: how the costs are shared

Since the law of 28 November 2024, buildings with central heating have shared energy costs according to each home's consumption. Here are the rules, explained simply.

Who must install meters?

In a co-ownership, the owners' association is responsible for installing an individual meter for each home supplied by the building's heating, cooling or hot water (article 2). If a meter cannot be fitted, heat cost allocators are installed on each radiator (article 4).

There are exceptions (article 3): technical impossibility, excessive cost compared with the energy savings, very low consumption and, for heating, buildings in thermal insulation classes A+ to D. For hot water in new homes whose building permit was applied for after 25 October 2020, technical impossibility cannot be invoked.

30% common costs, the rest by consumption

Energy costs (fuel, electricity or purchased heat) are split in two (article 9):

  • 30% make up the common costs, allocated as set out in the co-ownership rules;
  • the rest is allocated according to the readings of each home's meters or allocators. Homes at a thermal disadvantage can be taken into account.

If the meters were installed before the law came into force, the coefficient chosen at the time (between 0 and 50%) is kept, but the owners' association can switch to 30%.

Other heating costs

Boiler maintenance, operating the system and the electricity used by the equipment do not depend on the meters: they are allocated according to the co-ownership rules, like other co-ownership charges.

Hot water

The same rules apply to domestic hot water produced by a shared installation: 30% common costs for energy, the rest according to each home's hot water meters.

A note for each co-owner, with the meeting notice

With the notice of the general meeting that approves the accounts, the syndic sends each co-owner an information note on their home's heating and hot water consumption (article 10). The co-owner passes it on to their tenant within a month. Each person only has access to the data that concerns them (article 11).

Remote-readable meters by 1 January 2027 at the latest

Meters and allocators installed since the law came into force must be readable remotely, without entering the homes. Older devices must be made remotely readable or replaced by 1 January 2027 at the latest, unless this is technically impossible or the cost is disproportionate (article 6). With remote-readable meters, the syndic sends co-owners a monthly consumption note, which they forward to their occupants.

What mäisyndic does

Frequently asked questions

My flat has a poor orientation: will I pay more?

The law allows homes at a thermal disadvantage to be taken into account in the share allocated according to consumption, and in any case 30% of energy costs are still allocated according to the co-ownership rules.

I am a tenant: who gives me the consumption note?

Your landlord. They receive it from the syndic with the general meeting notice and must pass it on to you within a month (article 10 of the law of 28 November 2024).

What happens if a meter breaks down?

The home's consumption is estimated, for example based on a comparable period or the building's average consumption, then allocated in the same way as the others.
