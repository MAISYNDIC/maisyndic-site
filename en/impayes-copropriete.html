Unpaid charges: how the syndic recovers them

When one co-owner does not pay, the whole building's cash position suffers. Here are the steps provided for in Luxembourg, from the first reminder to the statutory mortgage.

Why you need to act quickly

Charges pay for insurance, energy, maintenance and the works fund. A late payment forces the co-ownership to dip into its cash, at the expense of those who do pay. The syndic must therefore follow up every late payment straight away and keep proof of every step taken.

Friendly reminders

At mäisyndic, a first reminder is sent 15 days after the due date of the call for funds (or after it was sent, if it went out later), then a second one at 30 days, by email or by post as the co-owner prefers, in their language. Before sending any reminder, we check that no transfer has arrived without a reference: we do not chase someone who has paid.

The formal notice

If the payment is still late, the syndic sends a formal notice, preferably by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt: this proves it was received. At mäisyndic, it is sent at 45 days and gives 15 days to pay.

It has two important effects:

  • it starts interest running at the statutory rate, unless the co-ownership rules provide otherwise (article 26 of the Grand-Ducal regulation of 13 June 1975);
  • it opens the way to the owners' association's statutory mortgage, which can only be registered after a formal notice has had no effect.

Recovery costs

No law or regulation automatically makes the co-owner in arrears pay the costs of reminders or of the formal notice. These costs can only be recharged to them if the co-ownership rules provide for it or if the general meeting has decided so. Some courts have already refused costs charged without one of these bases.

The owners' association's statutory mortgage

The law of 16 May 1975 (article 24) secures all the owners' association's claims against a co-owner with a statutory mortgage on their unit:

  • it is registered after a formal notice has had no effect, and ranks from the day it is registered;
  • the syndic can have it registered, then release it once the debt is paid, without going through the general meeting;
  • it cannot be taken for sums that have been due for more than five years.

The same claims also benefit from the landlord's lien (article 24 of the law of 16 May 1975, which refers to article 2102, 1° of the Civil Code): on the contents of the home or, if it is let unfurnished, on the rent owed by the tenant.

Going to court

If nothing changes, the syndic, who represents the owners' association in court, can ask the court to order the co-owner to pay, usually with the help of a lawyer. To recover charges, the syndic does not need authorisation from the general meeting (article 14, point 5, of the law of 16 May 1975). The judgment then allows the debt to be enforced, for example against the debtor's assets or income.

If the flat is sold

A sale does not wipe out the debt. The notary asks for a certificate from the syndic; without it, the syndic can object to the payment of the price within 8 days of the notice of transfer. The buyer is also jointly and severally liable with the seller for charges still due for the closed financial year and the months of the current one. Our guide to selling a flat.

Struggling to pay?

Let your syndic know as soon as possible. A payment plan is often possible, and paying quickly avoids interest, any costs and the formal notice. In your online portal, you can see at any time what you owe and what you have paid.

Frequently asked questions

Is late-payment interest automatic?

No. It runs from the formal notice sent by the syndic, at the statutory rate, unless the co-ownership rules provide for different interest (article 26 of the Grand-Ducal regulation of 13 June 1975).

Can the syndic go after the tenant?

The debt remains the owner's. But if the home is let unfurnished, the owners' association's lien covers the rent owed by the tenant (article 24 of the law of 16 May 1975 and article 2102 of the Civil Code).

Is a general meeting vote needed to register the mortgage?

No. The syndic has the authority to have the statutory mortgage registered in favour of the owners' association, then to release it once the debt has been paid off, without involving the general meeting.
