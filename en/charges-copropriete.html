Co-ownership charges

What you pay, how it is calculated and how to check it: thousandths, forecast budget, calls for funds, annual statement and works fund.

Key points

  • Charges are split according to the thousandths set by the co-ownership rules.
  • Common equipment, such as the lift, can follow a different key, based on how useful it is to each unit.
  • The budget is voted at the general meeting; the syndic calls for advance payments, often every quarter.
  • Each year, the accounts are presented to the meeting and the difference from your advance payments is settled.
  • Changing the allocation of charges requires unanimity as a rule.

What do the charges cover?

Co-owners pay together for the preservation, maintenance and administration of the common parts, and for running the common equipment. In practice: cleaning and electricity for the common parts, building insurance, maintenance of the lift and communal heating, minor repairs, the syndic's fees. On top of this come works voted by the general meeting and the annual contribution to the works fund.

How charges are split: thousandths

Each unit corresponds to a share of the common parts, set by the co-ownership rules, usually in thousandths. Where the rules are not specific, the share is proportional to the relative value of each private part. That value is assessed when the co-ownership is created, based on the part's composition, floor area and location, regardless of how it is used.

Charges for the preservation, maintenance and administration of the common parts are split according to these shares. For common equipment (lift, communal heating, intercom…), other criteria may be set; without a specific clause, each unit contributes according to how useful the equipment is to it. Depending on the rules, a ground-floor shop may therefore pay less for the lift.

Heating, cooling and hot water: since the law of 28 November 2024 and its Grand-Ducal regulation on individual metering, part of these costs must, in the buildings concerned, be allocated according to each unit's measured consumption. We check what applies to your building.

A common part that the rules reserve for the use of a single co-owner, such as a terrace or a garden, is maintained at that co-owner's expense. The co-ownership rules may provide otherwise, for example for the building's structure: check yours.

Example: an annual budget of €24,000 and a flat with 85 thousandths out of 1,000. This unit's share of the general charges is 24,000 × 85 / 1,000 = €2,040 a year, or €510 a quarter if advance payments are called quarterly.

Can the allocation be changed?

As a rule, the allocation of charges can only be changed unanimously. The law provides a few exceptions:

  • the meeting may bring the allocation rules into line with the criteria set by law (relative value of the units, usefulness of the equipment), by a majority of the votes of all co-owners;
  • when it votes on works, an acquisition or a sale, it may make the change to the allocation that this requires, by the same majority as that decision;
  • where charges concern only some co-owners, only those co-owners vote on how they are allocated.

If no decision is made, any co-owner may ask the District Court to set the new allocation. A change voted by the meeting may also be challenged in court within two months of its notification.

Forecast budget and calls for funds

The syndic prepares the forecast budget, which the general meeting votes on. The syndic keeps separate accounts for each co-ownership, showing the position of each co-owner. To fund expenses, the syndic may call for:

  • the permanent cash advance provided for in the co-ownership rules;
  • advance payments on the voted budget, on the dates and within the limits set by the Grand-Ducal regulation of 13 June 1975, the co-ownership rules or the general meeting (most often every quarter);
  • special advance payments for works and decisions voted by the meeting;
  • the annual contribution to the works fund.

All sums received are paid without delay into a bank account opened in the name of the owners' association. In an emergency, the syndic takes the measures needed to safeguard the building and informs the co-owners.

The annual statement: what to check

At the end of the financial year, the syndic presents the income and expenditure account, the statement of debts and receivables and the cash position to the meeting. These documents are sent at the latest with the meeting notice. Your individual statement then compares your actual share of the expenses with the advance payments you made: the difference is either credited to you or requested from you.

A few points to check:

  • the thousandths and allocation keys applied to your unit;
  • the advance payments you actually made;
  • expenses that are unusual or have risen sharply compared with the previous year;
  • if in doubt, the corresponding invoices.

The owners' council oversees the syndic's management, in particular the accounts and the allocation of expenses; its members may inspect all documents at the syndic's office. At mäisyndic, every paid invoice appears in the online portal, with a copy attached.

If payments are late

Unless the co-ownership rules say otherwise, sums owed bear interest at the statutory rate from the date of the formal notice sent by the syndic. The co-ownership's claims are secured by a statutory mortgage on the co-owner's unit, which the syndic may have registered after a formal notice has had no effect. The syndic may take legal action to recover these sums without prior authorisation from the meeting.

Selling a unit: who pays what?

When a unit is sold, the seller may give the notary a certificate from the syndic, less than one month old, confirming that they are up to date with payments to the co-ownership. Otherwise, the buyer must inform the syndic of the sale by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt. The syndic then has eight days to object to the payment of the price and obtain payment of the sums the seller still owes.

In all cases, the buyer is jointly and severally liable with the seller for charges still owed for the closed financial year and for the months of the current year. Contributions to the works fund, however, stay with the unit: they are not refunded to the seller.

The works fund

Since 1 August 2023, every co-ownership has paid into a works fund through a compulsory annual contribution. The meeting sets the amount by the article 15 majority, without going below the minimum per square metre set by the legislation, and the contribution is split according to each unit's share.
